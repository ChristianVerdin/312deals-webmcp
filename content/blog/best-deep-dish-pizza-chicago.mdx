## The best deep dish pizza in Chicago, ranked

If you only have room for one deep dish in Chicago, here's where to get it, ranked by what locals and visitors actually order:

1. **Lou Malnati's**, the consensus number one. A flaky, almost buttery "buttercrust," a lean sausage patty, and sweet tomato on top. The most consistently great deep dish in the city, with locations across Chicago and the suburbs.
2. **Pequod's Pizza** (Lincoln Park), the local cult favorite, famous for the caramelized cheese "crust ring" baked around the edge. The pick if you want the one pie Chicagoans rave about.
3. **Gino's East**, the golden cornmeal crust and graffiti-covered walls; a River North original known since the 1960s.
4. **Giordano's**, the famous *stuffed* pizza, with a second layer of dough sealing in a deep well of cheese under the sauce. Touristy, but an iconic Chicago bite.
5. **Pizzeria Uno** (River North), where deep dish was reportedly invented in 1943; the historic first bite, with Pizzeria Due a block away.

**Short on time?** Lou Malnati's is the safe pick. **Want the local favorite?** Go to Pequod's.

## The local secret: tavern-style thin crust

Here's the thing most visitors don't know, **Chicagoans eat way more tavern-style thin crust than deep dish.** It's the cracker-thin, square-cut ("party cut") pizza that shows up at every neighborhood bar and corner spot. Vito & Nick's on the South Side is the temple, but nearly every tavern in the city has its version. If you want to eat like a local, that's the move.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best deep dish pizza in Chicago?** Lou Malnati's is the most widely recommended, a flaky buttercrust, a lean sausage patty, and sweet tomato on top, with consistent quality across its Chicago locations. Pequod's in Lincoln Park is the local cult favorite for its caramelized cheese crust ring.

**Where do locals go for deep dish in Chicago?** Locals lean toward Pequod's and Lou Malnati's over the more tourist-heavy chains, though most Chicagoans actually eat tavern-style thin crust far more often than deep dish.

**Who invented deep dish pizza?** Deep dish was reportedly invented at Pizzeria Uno in River North in 1943.

**What is the difference between deep dish and stuffed pizza?** Deep dish has a tall, buttery crust with cheese on the bottom and chunky tomato sauce on top. Stuffed pizza, Giordano's is the famous example, adds a second layer of dough over the cheese before the sauce, making the pie even deeper.
